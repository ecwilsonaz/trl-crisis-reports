### Q1 — Were any of the document's directives ever carried out? Was any staff member asked to move materials, take down a display, or reshelve anything, or has any branch reported being told to?
> No. The 'desired outcomes' are exactly that — the stated wishes of one board member. The board sets policy, and our executive director and professional staff are responsible for procedures and implementation. A single trustee has no authority to direct staff at all. The board only acts as a body, by majority vote. Even with this in mind, the board's job is to set policy, not to hand staff operational directives about specific materials or displays. As I've said, I trust the professionals to make those decisions within the parameters of our established policies, strategic plan, and institutional commitments. If the board ever wanted to change the policy framework itself, that would take a majority vote at a public meeting.
>
> This document has never been placed on a board agenda. I have not been made aware of any account of these outcomes being presented to staff as a directive and that would be outside any single trustee's authority.

### Q2 — Has the document been discussed among the board, formally or informally, and do you know of any trustee besides Mittge who supports its direction?
> This specific document has never been an official agenda item in my time on the board. Some of the underlying themes, not this rhetoric or framing, have surfaced periodically, primarily from Trustee Mittge himself, in public meetings and board workshops.
>
> Brian and I did meet informally the weekend before the document was leaked, after his conversation with Andrea. I knew that meeting had happened, but I didn't have the full contents of the document until Brian and I spoke. It was two trustees talking and, as I've stated, I do not align with his desired outcomes.
>
> To my knowledge, no other board member has expressed support for these 'desired outcomes,' either publicly or to me directly.

### Q3 — Mittge chairs through this year, you take over as president next year. What happens to the document in the meantime, and what will you push for once chairing?
> I don't expect anything to come of the document. There isn't majority support for it on the board, and in my view it's at odds with the ALA Library Bill of Rights and TRL's own Mission, Vision, and Values.
>
> What I'll push for (now, and once I'm President)  is to keep stabilizing TRL to be the financially sound, operationally effective library system that our communities need. We adopted our Mission, Vision, and Values after a nearly year-long process involving both the board and staff, built on public input gathered through surveys. I stand by that work. The board has set clear priorities: improving financial management, revitalizing our organizational culture, strengthening relationships with our communities, making key decisions on a levy lid lift, and hiring a permanent executive director to drive all of this forward. Those are the real challenges in front of us, and that's where I intend to put my energy, because those are the things our library system needs to keep offering vital services to our communities for generations to come.
>
> I trust our librarians and staff to do the professional work of building and presenting our collections. My job as a trustee is governance and stewardship (setting policy and securing the resources they need), not running the shelves.

### Q4 — Do you anticipate any formal board action (statement, censure, leadership change), or is that not on the table? Does Mittge retain your confidence as president through the transition?
> That's a matter for the full board, and our June 24 business meeting is the first time we'll be together as a group since this surfaced, so I won't get ahead of that conversation. What I'll say for myself: Brian and I disagree, clearly and on the record, about this document. We also still share an important job to stabilize our five county library district and I intend to do my part of that work constructively, with every member of this board.

### Q5 — Swope says he'll fight any levy lid lift unless the library "changes course." As incoming president of a district that needs that levy, your response to a commissioner conditioning library funding on this? (Union AFSCME 3758 response also welcome.)
> The levy keeps libraries staffed and open across all five counties  including kids at summer library programs, seniors who need help with a form, people job-hunting on our computers, and rural patrons whose branch is the only public space for miles. That's what's on the ballot — not any one trustee's personal list of desired outcomes. Some people in our region share that view, and they're entitled to it. But a public library is funded to serve everyone, not to reflect any single viewpoint.
>
> Declining to single out and relocate materials by the identities they represent isn't refusing to "change course" — it's holding to our mission to connect our diverse communities to ideas, experiences, and each other. That's our mandate, not a failure to meet it.
>
> Conditioning funding for a five-county system on catering to one viewpoint at the expense of others asks the public to pay for a disagreement they didn't start. The people who'd feel the impact of a failed levy are those that need it the most including families, kids, seniors, and other vulnerable people across all five counties.
>
> I'll keep making the case for the levy precisely so we can staff this system properly, so our librarians and staff have the capacity for the outreach, programming, and planning our communities are asking for. That's not a library anyone should fear. It's a library that shows up for everyone.
>
> Our staff and their union have stood up for intellectual freedom and a library that serves everyone, and I share that commitment. I won't speak for the full board, but I'll say it for myself: a well-run library, open to all, is worth funding. The alternative is a loss for all of our communities.

### Q6 (June 23 follow-up) — Are you aware of a children's-area privacy pamphlet whose language was recently removed or changed? Mittge cites it as a concern already addressed.
