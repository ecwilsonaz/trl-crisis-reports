# Andrea Heisel (TRL interim director) — on-record clarification re: the LGBTQ-youth flyer privacy language

**Source:** Email from Andrea Heisel to Eric Wilson, June 23, 2026, responding to a follow-up press question for the "desired outcomes" article (whether the children's-area pamphlet language was removed, when, and why). On the record; continuation of the channel through which Heisel provided written answers for this article. TRL email is a WA public record (chapter 42.56 RCW).

**Context:** The line at issue — "No matter your age, you have the right to keep your library account private. Check with library staff anytime with questions about your account." — appeared on TRL's "Resources for LGBTQIA+ Kids & Families" flyer (interior panel, "Where are the books for kids like me?", June 2023 version). Mittge's "desired outcomes" document included a photo of the pamphlet, and his directive "no dividing kids from parents, no suggesting they hide things from them" refers to this line.

---

## Verbatim

> Thank you for the follow-up question, Mr. Wilson.
>
> In the document Trustee Mittge shared, there was a picture of the pamphlet, so I addressed that the language had been changed because the new version had recently been shared with me. No one directed the staff to make that change.
>
> The particular phrasing ("No matter your age, you have the right to keep your library account private") in this pamphlet has come up as an issue before (mostly via social media posts where people accused us of grooming children). I have had conversations with staff about the language and heard the need to balance the message of you can find the resources you need AND we do want parents to also be a part of that guidance if they so choose.
>
> The language change was made on 6/8 prior to my meeting with Trustees Mittge and Blanton on 6/12. The new language focuses on encouraging people to ask for help to find resources. Again, I did not direct staff to make those changes. They came up with this language on their own.
>
> It is true that a person's library account (even a minor's) is private unless they explicitly give approval to share information. When we sign kids and families up for a library card, we do go over this with them.
>
> Sincerely,
> Andrea
